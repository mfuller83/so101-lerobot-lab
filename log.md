## Training Environment Concept - 1/09/2026
The concept is to create a simple three-sided training cell consisting of a base, back wall, and left wall.
The robot will be mounted to the base in the front-right corner, facing approximately towards the back-left corner of the cell.
The enclosure will be constructed from approximately 6–12 mm MDF sheet. MDF is inexpensive, readily available, easy to manufacture, and should provide a consistent surface finish when painted. A uniform visual environment should also help reduce unnecessary variation in the camera images used for training.
The internal faces of the training cell will be painted grey to provide a neutral and consistent background.
### Lighting
Two LED lighting strips will be mounted to the walls to provide consistent illumination across the working area.
Maintaining repeatable lighting conditions should make the initial learning tasks easier by reducing visual variation between demonstrations.
Adjustable brackets will be designed for the lighting so that both the position and angle can be changed during development.
### Cameras
The training cell will be designed to support up to three cameras.
Initially, only one camera will be used. The first camera will be fixed to one of the walls and positioned to provide a clear view of the robot workspace.
As the project develops, additional cameras may be introduced to investigate the benefits of multiple viewpoints.
Possible future camera positions include:
Fixed front or side camera
Fixed overhead camera
Wrist-mounted camera
Adjustable camera brackets will be designed to allow both the position and viewing angle to be changed easily.
Levelling
It may also be beneficial to include the ability to level the complete training cell.
Simple adjustable levelling feet could be used for this purpose. However, their mounting requirements will need to be considered when selecting the final thickness and construction of the base.
### Cell Size
Initial recommendations for the training cell were approximately 800 × 800 mm.
This appears significantly larger than the usable reach of the SO-101, so a smaller 600 × 600 mm working area currently seems like a more practical starting point.
The final dimensions will be confirmed by carrying out a study of the robot's reachable workspace before the training cell is manufactured.
